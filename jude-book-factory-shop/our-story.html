Our story

Jude and the Book Factory

Jude started the Book Factory when he was just four years old and his idea was simple: get books to children.

He started by hiding his own books for others to find. Jude would pick out a book, label it and then go into full 007 mode as he set about creating the perfect book drop. Hiding a book was his gift to another child.

As word spread of what Jude was up to, people wanted to join in, so he asked them to be his "frends". Today more than 1,000 young bookworms hide their outgrown books for other children to find. There's no commitment: hide as many, or as few, books as you like.

Jude designed the Book Factory logo himself. He drew himself as a Willy Wonka-style figure in a big hat, and the blue is his hearing aids. He loved his yearly trip to pick sunflowers, which is why every new frend's welcome pack includes seeds to grow.

Jude was diagnosed with high-risk neuroblastoma and died in 2023, aged five. We are proud to carry on his mission to promote reading and to make sure children have access to books.

What we do

We give brand new books to children who need escape and adventure: in children's hospitals and hospices such as Sheffield Children's Hospital and Bluebell Wood, and to families affected by childhood cancer and grief. To mark what would have been Jude's sixth birthday in July 2024, we donated 1,140 brand new books in a single month.

We also run book hides and free book swap spaces, starting with the first children's book swap space at Annie Jude's in Hillsborough. We've donated Braille books and books for a local foodbank, gifted goody bags to children going through treatment, and funded creative workshops so children can explore their own imaginations.

Why we opened a shop

We buy lovely things from independent makers through the Faire wholesale marketplace, often at reduced value, and sell them on at fair prices. You get a great gift, and the profit goes straight back into books, goody bags and workshops for children who need a little magic.
